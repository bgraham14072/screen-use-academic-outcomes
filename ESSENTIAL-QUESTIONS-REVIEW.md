- **What could change the answer:** Independent trials of the same lessons with and without the digital feature, with comparable teaching and lasting learning measured outside the tool.

### Is reading on paper better than reading on a screen? When is print the better choice?

[Open question 2](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#q2-paper-reading). Research label: fairly consistent.

- **Evidence:** [R1 · Paper versus screen comprehension](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#record-R1); [Delgado et al. · Comparable reading texts](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#paper-delgado); [Clinton · Paper reading and genre limits](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#paper-clinton); [Furenes et al. · Reading features and adult support](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#reading-furenes).
- **Review flag:** The scoped comprehension finding is fairly consistent; universal format rules are not supported.
- **What could change the answer:** Well-controlled comparisons of current formats, with matched adult support and accessibility, showing different results for the same readers, texts and learning goals.

### Which e-book and reading-app features help, and which get in the way?

[Open question 3](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#q3-reading-features). Research label: mixed.

- **Evidence:** [Furenes et al. · Reading features and adult support](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#reading-furenes); [Takacs et al. · Multimedia and interactive features](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#reading-takacs); [Munzer et al. · Parent–toddler reading interactions](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#reading-munzer).
- **Review flag:** Mixed feature findings; existing evidence cannot rank individual current apps.
- **What could change the answer:** Trials that turn individual features on or off in otherwise identical books and measure comprehension, word learning and adult–child talk separately.

### How much does a teacher's or parent's involvement matter?

[Open question 4](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#q4-adult-support). Research label: mixed.

- **Evidence:** [S6 · Parent co-viewing and language](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#record-S6); [Furenes et al. · Reading features and adult support](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#reading-furenes); [Segal-Drori et al. · Adult-instructed e-book reading](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#reading-segal); [Munzer et al. · Parent–toddler reading interactions](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#reading-munzer).
- **Review flag:** Thin for the size of the adult-support contribution across ages and tools; comparisons are not consistently matched.
- **What could change the answer:** Trials that vary the type of adult instruction while holding the book and teaching time constant, with follow-up across age groups.

### Should students take notes by hand?

[Open question 5](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#q5-handwritten-notes). Research label: mixed.

- **Evidence:** [Mueller & Oppenheimer · Original longhand finding](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#notes-original); [Morehead et al. · Note-taking replication](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#notes-replication); [Flanigan et al. · Note-taking synthesis](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#notes-synthesis); [Van der Weel & Van der Meer · EEG, not achievement](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#notes-eeg).
- **Review flag:** Thin for younger pupils and accessibility needs; college findings are mixed.
- **What could change the answer:** Replicated school-age trials comparing note methods, with accessible options, equivalent note review and later tests of understanding.

## WELLBEING AND THE BIGGER PICTURE

### How much screen time at school is too much?

[Open question 6](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#q6-school-screen-time). Research label: limited.

- **Evidence:** [P1 · Limited school learning use](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#record-P1); [P2 · Limited school leisure use](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#record-P2); [P3 · Distraction by classmates’ devices](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#record-P3); [P4 · Heavier school learning use](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#record-P4); [P6 · Very high school leisure use](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#record-P6); [P7 · Limited versus heavy school leisure use](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#record-P7); [WHO · Sedentary behaviour and movement](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#wellbeing-movement); [Bettinger et al. · Randomized homework dosage](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#study-bettinger); [Steenbergen-Hu & Cooper · 2013 · Meta-analysis of controlled comparisons; not a single randomized trial.](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#study-steenbergen); [Dynarski et al. · 2007 · Teacher/classroom-cluster randomized controlled trial within schools; students were not randomly assigned to teachers.](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#study-federal2007); [Campuzano et al. · 2009 · Teacher/classroom-cluster randomized controlled trial within schools; follow-on analyses of the federal evaluation.](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#study-federal2009).
- **Review flag:** Thin for a causal, age-specific school-day limit. The randomized homework-dose study is not a trial of total daily school screen time.
- **What could change the answer:** School-based trials of different time limits with comparable teaching, tracking learning and wellbeing by age, purpose and out-of-school use.

### Does screen use affect sleep, attention or mental health? Is school use different from home use?

[Open question 7](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#q7-wellbeing). Research label: mixed.

- **Evidence:** [Dibben et al. · Sleep](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#wellbeing-sleep); [Thorell et al. · Attention](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#wellbeing-attention); [Sanders et al. · Mental health](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#wellbeing-mental-health); [Surgeon General · Social-media advisory](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#wellbeing-social-media); [Norwegian Institute of Public Health · Development](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#wellbeing-development); [Allcott et al. · Quasi-experimental phone-pouch study](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#study-allcott); [Vuorre & Przybylski · Population-level internet associations](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#study-vuorre); [Odgers & Jensen · Adolescent mental-health review](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#study-odgers).
- **Review flag:** Thin for a direct school-versus-home causal comparison. Ecological associations, adolescent reviews and quasi-experimental personal-phone restrictions answer different questions.
- **What could change the answer:** Studies separating school instruction from home leisure, with repeated measures and stronger causal designs that account for prior wellbeing and family circumstances.

### Are test scores falling because of classroom technology?

[Open question 8](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#q8-test-score-declines). Research label: not yet studied.

- **Evidence:** [The strongest case for pulling back, and what the evidence says about it.](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#pulling-back); [P3 · Distraction by classmates’ devices](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#record-P3); [P4 · Heavier school learning use](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#record-P4); [D1 · Mobile literacy & numeracy lessons](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#record-D1); [D2 · Higher-risk-study sensitivity check](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#record-D2).
- **Review flag:** No direct test in the dashboard of the causal explanation for national score declines.
- **What could change the answer:** Credible policy or rollout comparisons that isolate changes in classroom technology from other changes and track achievement before and after.

### What did Sweden do, and has it worked?

[Open question 9](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#q9-sweden). Research label: not yet studied.

- **Evidence:** [The strongest case for pulling back, and what the evidence says about it.](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#pulling-back).
- **Review flag:** No evaluation of the Swedish policy’s effects is included in the dashboard.
- **What could change the answer:** An independent evaluation of implemented Swedish policies, with comparison groups and learning, wellbeing and equity outcomes over time.

### If New York banned phones, should the same rules apply to school devices?

[Open question 10](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#q10-phones-and-school-devices). Research label: limited.

- **Evidence:** [The strongest case for pulling back, and what the evidence says about it.](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#pulling-back); [P3 · Distraction by classmates’ devices](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#record-P3); [FOCUS Act crosswalk · Requirements and exceptions](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#focus-crosswalk); [Allcott et al. · Quasi-experimental phone-pouch study](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#study-allcott).
- **Review flag:** Thin for transferring phone-policy effects to school-issued instructional devices. Allcott is not an RCT or a peer-reviewed journal article.
- **What could change the answer:** Evaluations of New York’s phone policy and separate school-device policies, with comparable schools and measures of learning, wellbeing and access.

## EQUITY AND ACCESS

### Could limiting school technology widen the digital divide?

[Open question 11](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#q11-digital-divide). Research label: not yet studied.

- **Evidence:** [Could limiting school technology widen the digital divide?](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#digital-divide); [Roschelle et al. · 2016; Murphy et al. · 2020 · School-cluster randomized controlled trial; the later publication reanalyzes the same Maine trial.](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#study-assistments-maine); [Feng, Huang & Collins · 2024 · School-cluster randomized controlled trial with a later follow-up of the same student cohort.](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#study-assistments-nc); [Steenbergen-Hu & Cooper · 2013 · Meta-analysis of controlled comparisons; not a single randomized trial.](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#study-steenbergen).
- **Review flag:** No direct study in the cited set tests the equity effects of removing school technology.
- **What could change the answer:** Comparative evaluations of actual school restrictions, measuring access, digital skills, learning and wellbeing across income groups and existing home resources.

### What about students with disabilities and English learners?

[Open question 12](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#q12-access-needs). Research label: limited.

- **Evidence:** [N1 · Elementary second-language reading](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#record-N1); [FOCUS Act crosswalk · Requirements and exceptions](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#focus-crosswalk); [AI4ExceptionalEd · Screening and individualized support](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#research-screener); [CELaRAI and AIRE · Beginning reading](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#research-aire); [Could limiting school technology widen the digital divide?](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#digital-divide); [Fernández-Batanero et al. · Assistive technology and inclusion](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#study-fernandez).
- **Review flag:** Thin for tool-specific causal effects for particular disability and language groups. The descriptive assistive-technology review has reporting inconsistencies and does not determine legal eligibility.
- **What could change the answer:** Accessible, independently evaluated studies of specific tools for clearly described disability and language groups, measuring learning, participation and unintended barriers.

[Open question 13](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#q13-ai-reading). Research label: not yet studied.

- **Evidence:** [CELaRAI and AIRE · Beginning reading](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#research-aire); [Safeguards and possible unintended effects](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#research-safeguards); [L1 · Technology-delivered literacy instruction](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#record-L1); [Segal-Drori et al. · Adult-instructed e-book reading](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#reading-segal); [Létourneau et al. · K–12 intelligent tutoring review](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#study-letourneau); [Muralidharan, Singh & Ganimian · 2019 · Individual randomized controlled trial: voucher lottery; intention-to-treat effect of the offer.](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#study-mindspark); [Roschelle et al. · 2016; Murphy et al. · 2020 · School-cluster randomized controlled trial; the later publication reanalyzes the same Maine trial.](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#study-assistments-maine); [Escueta, Nickow, Oreopoulos & Quan · 2020 · Review of experimental studies: randomized controlled trials and regression-discontinuity designs, not a pooled meta-analysis.](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#study-escueta).
- **Review flag:** No completed classroom-achievement trial for the proposed implementation is included. General intelligent-tutoring findings are not early-literacy AI validation.
- **What could change the answer:** Independent classroom trials of a defined AI tool plus teacher instruction versus equally supported instruction without it, with lasting reading outcomes and safety checks.

### If an AI tool can spot reading difficulties, does that mean it improves reading?

[Open question 14](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#q14-screening-and-learning). Research label: limited.

- **Evidence:** [AI4ExceptionalEd · Screening and individualized support](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#research-screener); [FOCUS Act crosswalk · Requirements and exceptions](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#focus-crosswalk); [Safeguards and possible unintended effects](https://bgraham14072.github.io/screen-use-academic-outcomes/#research-safeguards).
- **Review flag:** Thin for the proposed tools’ detection-to-learning pathway; validation of a measure is not an achievement trial.
- **What could change the answer:** Studies that validate detection in the intended learners and compare reading outcomes when teachers use the results versus usual assessment and support.

- **What could change the answer:** Independent, replicated studies of the actual tool and version, with longer follow-up, reading transfer, subgroup results, harms reporting and privacy audits.

## Editorial safeguards

Labels are plain-language summaries, not formal quality grades. Intervention findings, observational associations and ecological patterns remain distinct, and the new general intelligent-tutoring review does not validate early-literacy AI implementations.
