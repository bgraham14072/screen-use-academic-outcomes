# Screen Use & Academic Outcomes: Source Verification Report

Brian S. Graham, Ed.D., Donna DeSiato, Ed.D., Kusum Sinha, Ed.D., Jared Bloom, Ed.D., and Matthew Gaven

17 November 2026

We offer this dashboard to help educators and policymakers examine when technology strengthens learning, when a non-digital approach may be preferable, and what evidence should guide those decisions. This report explains the provenance, interpretation and limitations of the evidence presented; it is not an exhaustive systematic review.

## Scope and reporting standards

We distinguish intervention effects from observational associations and keep different effect-size scales in separate comparisons. We do not add overlapping samples, infer missing contrasts by subtracting plotted means, or calculate unreported confidence intervals from standard errors.

The numerical register concerns academic outcomes. Wellbeing findings and emerging research are presented separately, and neither a promising research aim nor a positive academic finding establishes product safety or effectiveness.

PISA score differences are displayed as whole points, consistent with OECD’s presentation; downloadable CSV files retain the exact stored values. Other scales retain their source precision, and NR means not reported or not verified in the accessible original material, not zero.

## Interpreting the PISA time-band pattern

OECD reports that students who used digital devices for leisure at school for up to one hour per day scored 49 mathematics points higher than students reporting five to seven hours, after accounting for students’ and schools’ socioeconomic profile; record P7 reproduces that published contrast exactly ([OECD press release, 5 December 2023](https://www.oecd.org/en/about/news/press-releases/2023/12/decline-in-educational-performance-only-partly-attributable-to-the-covid-19-pandemic.html)). OECD also states that learning outcomes were often better among students reporting one to five hours of learning use than among those reporting none; this remains a narrative finding rather than an invented pooled numerical contrast ([OECD, PISA 2022: Insights and Interpretations](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/about/programmes/edu/pisa/publications/PISA%202022%20Insights%20and%20Interpretations.pdf)).

Figure II.5.14 presents average mathematics scores across the time bands before socioeconomic adjustment: learning-use averages are higher in the limited-use bands than with no use, while leisure-use averages peak at up to one hour, fall through the five-to-seven-hour band, then rise somewhat beyond seven hours while remaining below limited use ([OECD Volume II, Chapter 5, Figure II.5.14](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/pisa-2022-results-volume-ii_a97db61c-en/full-report/component-12.html)). Those figure means must not be confused with the adjusted 49-point contrast, and neither analysis establishes causation or a recommended daily dose ([OECD press release](https://www.oecd.org/en/about/news/press-releases/2023/12/decline-in-educational-performance-only-partly-attributable-to-the-covid-19-pandemic.html), [OECD Chapter 5](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/pisa-2022-results-volume-ii_a97db61c-en/full-report/component-12.html)).

## Discrepancies and unresolved limits

- **T1:** The science value is supported only by a secondary summary, not verification of the original science table. The restricted publisher abstract reports an overall review result but does not expose the science subgroup; this estimate should not be treated as original-publication-verified evidence ([Liao publisher page](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1300/J025v24n03_04), [Scottish Government review](https://www.gov.scot/publications/literature-review-impact-digital-technology-learning-teaching/pages/4/)).
- **S3:** Xie’s original review concerns preschool children aged 0–5 and reports N 4,206, versus 5,810 in Sanders’ reanalysis; neither sample was substituted or added to the other ([Xie](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6305619/), [Sanders](https://jyx.jyu.fi/bitstreams/2742f046-d6a7-45a6-b556-da740ddd8ae4/download)).
- **S4:** The underlying review includes university students and motivation/attitude outcomes, so the old youth label should not be read as child-specific achievement evidence; its reported N 1,560 also differs from Sanders’ N 1,474 ([Tekedere Table 1](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1118774.pdf), [Sanders](https://jyx.jyu.fi/bitstreams/2742f046-d6a7-45a6-b556-da740ddd8ae4/download)).
- **L2/L3 and P3:** Missing original CIs and the shared PISA source’s wrong chapter locator were recovered and flagged, not silently changed in the original records ([Dahl-Leonard Table 7](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s11423-024-10354-0.pdf), [OECD Chapter 3](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/pisa-2022-results-volume-ii_a97db61c-en/full-report/component-10.html)).
- **G1:** Conflicting science/math subgroup counts and publication-bias reporting remain in the publication; the overall estimate must not become a “best intervention” recommendation ([Behnamnia](https://revistas.uma.es/index.php/innoeduca/article/download/19694/20777/108443)).
- **Emerging research:** Published technical, survey and screening-validation outputs exist, but none establishes the efficacy of every proposed classroom implementation. We distinguish those results from completed achievement trials of the proposed classroom tools ([CELaRAI publications](https://earlyliteracyai.org/publications-research/), [AutoRSR original publication](https://doi.org/10.1044/2026_LSHSS-25-00268)).

## Implications for interpretation

We consider evidence of instructional benefit alongside distraction, recreational exposure, wellbeing concerns and the merits of non-digital learning. A favorable average does not validate every product, and an unfavorable association does not establish the same effect for every classroom use.

Research awards, planned pilots and laboratory measures are not substitutes for demonstrated classroom outcomes. We therefore emphasize adult guidance, meaningful alternatives, protected experiences and evidence of learning when considering a proposed use.

The policy crosswalk distinguishes legislative text from educational interpretation. Universal screening before disability identification remains a question for explicit guidance, not a claimed exemption; this is an educational analysis, not legal advice ([S10685](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/S10685)).
