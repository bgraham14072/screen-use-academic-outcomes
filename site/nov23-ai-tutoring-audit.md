# AI Tutoring & Instructional Technology Packet Intake Audit (November 23, 2026)

This audit documents the primary-source verification, duplicate screening, vendor-governance screening and dashboard placement for every item in the November 19, 2026 research packet (*Research on Instructional Technology and AI Tutoring*). Every number added to the dashboard comes directly from the original publication rather than the summary packet. The original 29-record numerical evidence register (`data.js`) and prior register extensions (`P4`-`P7`, `R1`, `M1`, `M2`) remain untouched.

## Primary-Source Verification Findings and Packet Discrepancies

### 1. Outhwaite, Faulder, Gulliford & Pitchford (2019; online June 25, 2018)
- **Primary links**: [APA DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000286) · [UCL Discovery open PDF](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10058087/1/Raising%20early%20achievement%20in%20math%20with%20interactive%20apps.pdf)
- **Verified values**: `461` children aged `4-5` randomized across `12` UK schools, `452` pretested, and `389` posttested across `11` schools (`126` Group 1 supplemental app, `131` Group 2 time-equivalent app replacement, `132` Group 3 standard-practice control; `63` absent at posttest including `30` on a school field trip). On PTM5 gain scores (p. 291): Group 1 vs Group 3 `d = 0.31, 95% CI [0.06, 0.55]`, `t(256) = 2.46, p = 0.015` (`n = 258`); Group 2 vs Group 3 `d = 0.21, 95% CI [-0.03, 0.46]`, `t(261) = 1.74`, one-tailed `p = 0.042` (`n = 263`); Group 1 vs Group 2 `d = 0.08, 95% CI [-0.17, 0.33]`, `t(255) = 0.64, p = 0.525`.
- **Discrepancies caught**: The packet citation listed pages `284-298` (actual journal issue pagination is `284-297`) and summarized the replacement arm as significant without noting that significance rested on a directional one-tailed test (`p = 0.042`) while the printed two-tailed 95% confidence interval `[-0.03, 0.46]` crosses zero. Both `M3` and `M4` are entered side by side so the supplemental gain and the two-tailed-uncertain replacement contrast carry equal weight. Both overlap Dorris et al. (2024) (`D1`/`D2`).

### 2. Ma, Adesope, Nesbit & Liu (2014)
- **Primary links**: [APA DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0037123) · [APA open PDF](https://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/features/edu-a0037123.pdf)
- **Verified values**: `107` independent effect sizes across `14,321` learners. Table 1 (p. 907) overall random-effects `g = 0.41, 95% CI [0.34, 0.48]` (fixed-effect `0.36 [0.32, 0.39]`, `Q(106) = 390.52`, `I-squared = 73%`). Random-effects comparator subgroups (Table 1, p. 907): large-group human instruction `g = 0.44 [0.35, 0.53]` (`k = 66, N = 11,296`), non-ITS computer instruction `g = 0.57 [0.34, 0.79]` (`k = 15, N = 1,034`), textbooks or workbooks `g = 0.36 [0.18, 0.53]` (`k = 17, N = 1,403`), small-group human instruction `g = 0.05 [-0.50, 0.61]` (`k = 4, N = 184`), individual human tutoring `g = -0.11 [-0.31, 0.10]` (`k = 5, N = 404`). Table 3 (p. 909): `60` of `107` effect sizes are postsecondary (`N = 6,767, g = 0.43 [0.33, 0.53]`) and `19` are elementary grades K-5 (`N = 1,496, g = 0.31 [0.16, 0.47]`).
- **Discrepancy caught**: Inside the original publication itself, the abstract on p. 901 prints `.42` and `.35` for large-group instruction and textbooks/workbooks (matching fixed-effect model figures), whereas Results p. 906 and Table 1 p. 907 print random-effects `0.44` and `0.36`. Both `M5` (overall `g = +0.41`) and `M6` (versus individual human tutoring `g = -0.11`) are entered in the register to prevent selective comparator claims.

### 3. Bastani, Bastani, Sungu, Ge, Kabakcı & Mariman (2025)
- **Primary links**: [PNAS DOI](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2422633122) · [PMC open full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12232635/) · [Aug. 20, 2025 affiliation correction](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2518204122)
- **Verified values**: Nearly `1,000` high-school math students across about `50` regular grades 9-11 classes in Turkey (`2,848` student-session observations across four 90-minute sessions, fall 2023). Table 1 normalized `0-1` OLS with classroom-clustered SEs: assisted practice `GPT Base +0.137 (SE = 0.031, +48%)` and `GPT Tutor +0.361 (SE = 0.032, +127%)` vs `0.284` control; unaided closed-book exam `GPT Base -0.054 (SE = 0.022, p < 0.05, -17%)` and `GPT Tutor -0.004 (SE = 0.013, null)` vs `0.321` control. `GPT Base` answered practice items accurately `51%` of the time (`42%` logical errors, `8%` arithmetic errors), and `67%` of first interactions across Session 1 problems in `GPT Base` were superficial versus `37%` in `GPT Tutor`.

### 4. Kestin, Miller, Klales, Milbourne & Ponti (2025)
- **Primary links**: [Scientific Reports open access](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-97652-6) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-97652-6)
- **Verified values**: `194` eligible Harvard University undergraduates (`233` enrolled in Physical Sciences 2, fall 2023) in a two-lesson peer-group crossover RCT. Post-test medians: `4.5` (`N = 142`) with `PS2 Pal` vs `3.5` (`N = 174`) in active-learning class from `2.75` (`N = 316`) pre-test baseline (`Mann-Whitney z = -5.6, p < 10^-8`); median time `49` minutes (`70%` under `60` minutes) vs `60`-minute class; OLS regression `0.63` SD and quantile regression `0.73` to `1.3` SD (95% CIs NR in main text); engagement `4.1` vs `3.6` (`p < 0.0001`), motivation `3.4` vs `3.1` (`p < 0.001`), enjoyment and growth mindset null. Prominently labeled **Higher education** so it is never conflated with K-5 classrooms.

### 5. Oreopoulos & Low (August 2026)
- **Primary links**: [NBER Working Paper 35620](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35620) · [NBER PDF](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w35620/w35620.pdf) · [AEA RCT Registry AEARCTR-0013519](https://www.socialscienceregistry.org/trials/13519)
- **Verified values**: Non-peer-reviewed working paper; two-year grade-within-school cluster RCT across `18` Hamilton County, Tennessee middle schools (`53` clusters: `28` treated, `25` control). Table 5 (`6,902` student-terms across 5 terms): `+1.264` MAP math percentile ranks/term (`SE = 0.597`; `+1.216`, `SE = 0.568` full controls; abstract rounds to `1.3`), `+0.758` RIT (`SE = 0.379`), `+0.040` population SD/term (`SE = 0.019`). Table 7 (`2,759` student-years): Year 1 `+0.020` population SD (`SE = 0.035`, null), Year 2 `+0.084` population SD (`SE = 0.041, p < 0.05`; `+0.116` RTI control SD, `SE = 0.053`; participation `0.61`, implied participant effect `+0.142`, rounded to `0.14` in abstract), stacked `+0.062` population SD (`SE = 0.035, p < 0.10`). Chatbot engagement: `96%` tried Khanmigo once, median student messaged it on `1/3` of practice days and in `17%` of mistake sessions (`1.7` chats/wk), and only `14.5%` of messages contained a math question or step of reasoning (`~$15`/student/year). Also discloses Oreopoulos's prior uncompensated 2021-2024 service on Khan Academy's informal research advisory committee reported in PNAS `10.1073/pnas.2604984123`.

### 6. Wang, Ribeiro, Robinson, Loeb & Demszky (November 2025)
- **Primary links**: [EdWorkingPaper 24-1056 DOI](https://doi.org/10.26300/81nh-8262) · [November 2025 PDF](https://edworkingpapers.com/sites/default/files/ai24_1054_v2.pdf) · [Stanford NSSA summary](https://nssa.stanford.edu/studies/tutor-copilot-human-ai-approach-scaling-real-time-expertise)
- **Verified values**: Non-peer-reviewed working paper; 7-week tutor-level RCT with FEV Tutor in grades 3-6 virtual math remediation across `9` Title I schools (`783` active tutors: `388` treatment, `395` control, from `900` initially randomized and `872` surveyed; `1,013` participating students of `1,534` eligible; `4,136` sessions; `350,000+` messages; `~$20`/tutor/year API cost). Unconditional session exit-ticket mastery rose `+4` percentage points (`+0.040, SE = 0.015, Romano-Wolf p = 0.010`, from `61.7%` to `65.7%`; conditional `+3.1` pp, `SE = 0.014, RW p = 0.040`), with `+9` pp (`56%` to `65%`) for lower-rated tutors and `+7` pp (`61%` to `68%`) for less-experienced tutors; participation points and all five student survey items were null; EOY NWEA MAP math was not directly estimated (`0.024` SD back-of-the-envelope projection only). Flags PDF header number `24-1054_v2` vs official DOI `24-1056`, earlier arXiv `2410.03017` randomized pool (`900` tutors / `~1,800` students), and first author part-time integration work with FEV Tutor (p. 17).

### 7. Withheld Study: Eames, Brunskill, Yamkovenko, Weatherholtz & Oreopoulos (2026)
- **Primary links**: [PNAS full text PMC12773755](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12773755/) · [PNAS DOI](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2507708123) · [March 4, 2026 PNAS Correction PMC12974481](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12974481/)
- **Reason withheld**: Excluded from both the numerical register and narrative study cards under the dashboard's standing source-governance rule barring vendor-produced or vendor-coauthored product efficacy research. Two of the five authors (Bogdan Yamkovenko and Kodi Weatherholtz) are employees of Efficacy Research at Khan Academy who co-designed the study and control the proprietary non-public Khan Academy and NWEA dataset, and the March 4, 2026 PNAS Correction adds that Emma Brunskill and Philip Oreopoulos served on Khan Academy's informal research advisory committee from 2021 to 2024 without financial compensation. In addition, the packet printed "about 214,000 students," whereas the article's actual sample is `203,465` unique grades 3-8 students across `213,907` student-year observations (`+0.031` SD at `6.6` annual hours, projected `+0.085` SD at `30` minutes/week, with only `10%` reaching recommended usage and `24%` logging zero usage).
