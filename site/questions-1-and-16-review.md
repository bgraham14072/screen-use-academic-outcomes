# Essential Questions 1 and 16: Review Draft

November 19, 2026. Full answer text from the public dashboard, retained as draft wording for co-author review. These are editorial summaries, not new pooled research results.

## Question 1: Does technology help children learn, and does it matter how it's used?

### Short answer

Technology can help when it provides well-targeted practice and useful feedback within a supported teaching approach, especially in mathematics. Some rigorous trials show meaningful gains, while broad personalization initiatives and generic software have produced modest, mixed or null results. The evidence supports choosing a specific instructional use, not assuming that devices or more screen time improve learning.

### How sure is the research?

mixed

### What the research shows

Mindspark combined practice at pupils’ actual learning level with instructor-supported study; Maine ASSISTments combined homework feedback, teacher reports and professional development. ([Muralidharan, Singh & Ganimian · 2019](https://econweb.ucsd.edu/~kamuralidharan/papers/Published%20Articles/Disrupting_education_AER.pdf); [Roschelle et al. · 2016; Murphy et al. · 2020](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1194398.pdf)) Both improved learning in randomized trials. ([Muralidharan, Singh & Ganimian · 2019](https://econweb.ucsd.edu/~kamuralidharan/papers/Published%20Articles/Disrupting_education_AER.pdf); [Roschelle et al. · 2016; Murphy et al. · 2020](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1194398.pdf)) North Carolina’s later follow-up was favorable, but its disrupted immediate assessment was not. ([Feng, Huang & Collins · 2024](https://www.wested.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/V07_ASSISTments-tech-report_FINAL-ADA.pdf); [Original 2023 conference paper: immediate null, pp. 9–10; not a journal article](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED631720.pdf)) Alongside these findings, the K–12 tutoring meta-analysis found small or uncertain average effects, federal software evaluations were mostly null with specific exceptions, and RAND’s later matched-comparison gains were smaller than its earlier results. ([Steenbergen-Hu & Cooper · 2013](https://dukespace.lib.duke.edu/bitstreams/f123780d-7d60-48c2-928d-8a4c402366b0/download); [Dynarski et al. · 2007](https://ies.ed.gov/sites/default/files/migrated/nces_pubs/ncee/pdf/20074005.pdf); [Campuzano et al. · 2009](https://ies.ed.gov/sites/default/files/migrated/nces_pubs/ncee/pubs/20094041/pdf/20094041.pdf); [Pane et al. · RAND 2015](https://www.rand.org/content/dam/rand/pubs/research_reports/RR1300/RR1365/RAND_RR1365.pdf); [Pane et al. · RAND 2017](https://www.rand.org/content/dam/rand/pubs/research_reports/RR2000/RR2042/RAND_RR2042.pdf)) Kulik’s favorable median was larger on local tests than on standardized tests. ([Kulik & Fletcher · 2016](https://www.ida.org/assets/2026/06/18023241/D-8391.pdf)) Ran’s function comparisons also suggest variation, not a causal ranking. ([Ran, Kim & Secada · 2022](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/epdf/10.1111/jcal.12611)) A professional-association evidence synthesis from the American Psychological Association likewise concludes that educational technology is neither inherently beneficial nor harmful: focused practice with feedback and adult involvement can support defined goals, whereas engagement, enjoyment and completion metrics are not learning, distracting design features and multitasking impede recall, and what technology replaces matters more than screen time alone. ([American Psychological Association · Sept. 3, 2026](https://www.apa.org/pubs/reports/childrens-adolescents-learning-educational-technology.pdf))

### What it doesn't show

These studies do not isolate teacher involvement as the causal explanation for differences across programs. They do not establish a universal benefit, a safe screen-time dose, direct transfer from low-income Delhi to New York, or efficacy of current generative AI for young children.

### What this means in a classroom

Start with a subject-specific learning need and pupils’ actual level. Compare the supported digital approach with strong non-digital teaching, and use off-screen show-me checks to verify whether students can remember, explain and apply learning after the app is closed.

### What would change this answer

Independent trials of the same lessons with and without the digital feature, with comparable teaching and lasting learning measured outside the tool.

## Question 16: How can a school tell whether a digital activity is worth it, and when should it scale back or stop?

### Short answer

Use a small, evaluated pilot rather than treating personalization as a guarantee of better learning. Check whether the activity fits pupils’ actual level, informs teacher action, targets a clear subject need, has implementation support, and improves learning on independent measures. Set review and stopping rules before starting, then scale back if learning goals are not met or important activities are displaced.

### How sure is the research?

limited

### What the research shows

The positive Mindspark and ASSISTments trials support examining five practical criteria: fit to actual student level; teachers acting on diagnostic information; a defined subject and learning goal; professional development and implementation support; and assessment independent of the vendor or practice task. ([Muralidharan, Singh & Ganimian · 2019](https://econweb.ucsd.edu/~kamuralidharan/papers/Published%20Articles/Disrupting_education_AER.pdf); [Roschelle et al. · 2016; Murphy et al. · 2020](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1194398.pdf)) These are proposed review criteria, not separately proven causal ingredients. Kulik’s test-alignment findings caution against relying only on local tests. ([Kulik & Fletcher · 2016](https://www.ida.org/assets/2026/06/18023241/D-8391.pdf)) RAND’s later modest or uncertain results and the federal software evaluations justify piloting and checking results rather than assuming broad personalization works. ([Pane et al. · RAND 2017](https://www.rand.org/content/dam/rand/pubs/research_reports/RR2000/RR2042/RAND_RR2042.pdf); [Dynarski et al. · 2007](https://ies.ed.gov/sites/default/files/migrated/nces_pubs/ncee/pdf/20074005.pdf); [Campuzano et al. · 2009](https://ies.ed.gov/sites/default/files/migrated/nces_pubs/ncee/pubs/20094041/pdf/20094041.pdf)) Bettinger shows that more assigned practice need not add benefit. ([Bettinger et al. · 2023](http://rfairlie.sites.luskin.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/32/2023/08/jpam-2023-marginal-returns-to-cal.pdf)) The American Psychological Association’s professional-association evidence synthesis adds that engagement, enjoyment and usage reports do not demonstrate learning; stronger review relies on independent validation and off-screen show-me checks of whether students can remember, explain and apply learning after the app is closed. ([American Psychological Association · Sept. 3, 2026](https://www.apa.org/pubs/reports/childrens-adolescents-learning-educational-technology.pdf)) Qualitative classroom observations from Instruction Partners reinforce that implementation depends on predictable teacher routines, flag risks from general-purpose chatbots, isolated skill drills and routine text releveling, and note how few student-facing AI products have independent subgroup outcome studies. ([Instruction Partners · Sept. 2026](https://ailearningtour.instructionpartners.org/about-the-tour))

### What it doesn't show

These criteria are not a validated checklist, a product certification or a universal stopping threshold. No cross-study comparison proves that teacher involvement alone explains why one program worked and another did not.

### What this means in a classroom

Specify the comparison, independent learning measure, subgroup checks and review date before the pilot, and ask students to show they can remember, explain and apply learning with the app closed. Adapt, reduce or stop the activity if gains stay confined to the platform, if it displaces valuable teaching, or if access, safety or privacy concerns persist.

### What would change this answer

Independent evaluations showing that specific decision and stopping rules reliably improve learning and wellbeing compared with ordinary school review.
